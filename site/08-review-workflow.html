Chapter 8 — The Review Workflow: Your Quality Gate

Every previous chapter ends with "you check it before learners see it." This chapter makes that check systematic — one workflow you run on every AI draft, in under ten minutes, so quality is a habit rather than a heroics.

The PASS-QC workflow

P — Purpose. Does this draft serve the stated learning objective? One sentence answer. If you can't say it, cut or refocus the draft before editing words.

A — Accuracy. All [VERIFY] tags cleared or rewritten as principles. Guideline/spec years confirmed. Numbers traced or deleted (Chapter 7).

S — Safety.

  • No patient data of any kind — automated check plus eyeball check (Chapter 1).
  • No clinical decision guidance slipped in (education only, always).
  • Fiction labeled on every teaching case and sim scenario.

S — Suitability. Right level for your actual learners? Activities doable in your actual room, with your actual prep budget? Timing survives a real clock (cut timing by half for any activity the AI proposed)?

Q — Quality of language. Read-aloud pass. Sentence you stumble on = rewrite shorter. Watch for AI tells: hedges stacked on hedges, empty adjectives ("crucial," "vital"), and the comma-splice flourish.

C — Care (fairness & accessibility). The Chapter 9 checklist: headings in order, alt text, no color-only cues, no timing traps, plain language, printable in grayscale.

Standing in ten minutes

StepTimeWhat you actually do
P1 minSay the objective out loud; compare to draft
A3 minClear every [VERIFY]; own-eyes guideline/year check
S2 minRun the checker script; skim for any real-data pattern
S2 minTiming/room/level sanity pass
Q1 minRead the first paragraph and every heading aloud
C1 minAccessibility quick-list

Ninety seconds per step is deliberate — this workflow only survives if it's cheap. Twenty minutes and you won't run it; ten minutes and you will.

What to do when the draft fails a step

Fail A → try fixing in the same chat: "Rewrite without any statistics or guideline names; state each as a principle." This conversion is almost always possible and usually improves teachability.

Fail S (safety) → never salvage. Cut the offending line entirely, no rewrite. Safety failures aren't editing problems; they're scope problems.

Fail S (suitability) → tell the AI the constraint you discovered ("my room has 6 tables and no tech") and regenerate only the activity section.

Fail Q → usually a signal the draft was too long. Cut 30% and the Q problems mostly vanish on their own.

Fail C → do the fix now, not the night before class. Accessibility fixes are fast when early, painful when late.

The "regenerate vs. edit" decision

The instinct is to keep editing a flawed draft. But AI regeneration is cheap:

  • Edit when the structure is right and the wording is close.
  • Regenerate when the structure is wrong (wrong activity shape, wrong scenario for your room), because wording-level fixes on a wrong structure eat hours.

Rule of thumb: if your third edit is targeting the same paragraph, stop and regenerate the section instead.

Version habits (lightweight, not bureaucratic)

Save every accepted draft with a suffix you can grep: _v2_final.

Write one line in a running note file per artifact:

2026-03-05  case_handoff_v2  — PASS-QC: A ✓ (2 numbers deleted), S ✓, 
              timing cut 25→10 min. Debuted with Tuesday cohort.

Three seconds to write; priceless when you teach the same material next semester and can't remember why v2 lost the statistics. (It was because of Chapter 7. You'll thank you.)

Team use: the peer-review ask

When a colleague reviews your AI-assisted material, ask exactly two questions:

  1. "Would you have caught the same [VERIFY] flags I did — did any slip?"
  2. "Is the fiction label visible enough that a tired you, at 9pm, won't read this as real?"

That's it. Two questions, not fifteen. Peer review of AI material fails when it becomes full-document line editing — which is your job, not theirs — or when it's skipped entirely.

Your standing reminder

Every AI draft is a gift of time-gained for review time spent. The workflow exists to spend that gained time on the parts only a human educator can judge: fit, tone, fairness, and truth. Run PASS-QC every time, and you'll rarely be caught out by the one draft that deserved it.
